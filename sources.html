HOW WE WORK

Sources & standards.

Good security reporting starts with evidence, context and clear attribution.

Preferred source types

Use primary sources wherever possible. These resources are starting points for research, not endorsements of every claim they publish.

01

Official vulnerability information

Check vulnerability identifiers, affected products and vendor guidance against primary records.

02

National and sector advisories

Government cybersecurity agencies and incident response organisations can provide timely, actionable context.

03

Research and vendor advisories

Read original research and vendor security notices. Confirm claims with independent evidence where possible.

Editorial principles

  • Attribute claims. Link to the original advisory, report or statement.
  • Separate facts from claims. Use careful wording for allegations, unconfirmed incidents and early investigations.
  • Include dates. Distinguish publication date from the date an incident occurred or was disclosed.
  • Explain impact. State affected versions, exposure conditions and mitigation steps when verified.
  • Correct transparently. Update material errors and explain meaningful changes.
  • Avoid unnecessary alarm. Do not imply a system is exploitable or a breach is confirmed without supporting evidence.

RESEARCH WORKFLOW

Before publishing

  1. Find the primary source.
  2. Cross-check key claims.
  3. Confirm dates and affected products.
  4. Write an original summary.
  5. Add mitigation and source links.
